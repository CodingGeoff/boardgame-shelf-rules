## Overview

Match colour or number, punish the next player with +2, +4, skip and reverse, and say UNO with one card left.

## Setup

1. Deal 7 each; flip a start card (redo if +4).

## How to play

1. Play a card matching colour, number or symbol, or a wild.
2. If you cannot, draw 1 and play it if possible.
3. Say UNO with one card left; if caught, draw 2.

## Game end & scoring

- First to empty their hand wins the round; play to points if desired.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** First to empty their hand wins the round; play to points if desired.
2. **What you do on a turn:** Play a card matching colour, number or symbol, or a wild.
3. **Setup check:** Deal 7 each; flip a start card (redo if +4).
4. **The one rule people forget:** Wild +4 officially only without a matching colour.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- Save +4 for the end.
- Use skip or reverse against a player about to go out.
- Keep varied colours.

## Common mistakes

- Wild +4 officially only without a matching colour.
- Stacking +2 is a house rule.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** big parties; icebreakers; newcomers and grandparents can learn it instantly
- **Not ideal for:** no obvious bad fit

## Advanced guide: teaching script, time budget, phase strategy, extra FAQ

> Summarised by the editor from the rules above for teaching and improving; the rulebook wins on conflicts.

### First-game time budget

- Teaching: about 5 min (weight ●).
- Play: 30–30 min; budget about 35 min for a first game.
- Total: about 40 min.

### Teaching script (read it aloud)

> Match colour or number, or draw. Action cards: Skip, Reverse, +2, Wild, +4. Shout 'UNO' at one card or draw 2 if caught. First out wins and scores others' hands.

### Strategy by phase

- **Early:** Hold Wilds and +4s.
- **Mid:** Track colours others lack.
- **Late:** Disrupt others near the end.

### Extra FAQ

- **Q: Can I play +4 anytime?**  
  A: Officially only without the current colour; it can be challenged.
- **Q: Can +2 stack?**  
  A: Not officially; common house rule.
