## Overview

Arrange black and white number tiles in ascending order as your code, then guess opponents' tiles; keep guessing while you are right.

## Setup

1. Each player draws 3–4 tiles (by player count) and arranges them face down in ascending order; black is lower than white for equal numbers.

## How to play

1. Draw a tile and insert it secretly in order.
2. Guess one opponent tile's number. Correct: it is revealed and you may guess again or stop (hiding your drawn tile).
3. Wrong: reveal the tile you just drew.

## Game end & scoring

- A player whose tiles are all revealed is out; the last player with hidden tiles wins.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** A player whose tiles are all revealed is out; the last player with hidden tiles wins.
2. **What you do on a turn:** Draw a tile and insert it secretly in order.
3. **Setup check:** Each player draws 3–4 tiles (by player count) and arranges them face down in ascending order; black is lower than white for equal numbers.
4. **The one rule people forget:** Equal numbers: black is placed left of white.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- Use revealed neighbours to narrow ranges.
- Stopping after one correct guess keeps your new tile hidden.
- Jokers (if used) can sit anywhere.

## Common mistakes

- Equal numbers: black is placed left of white.
- A wrong guess reveals your newly drawn tile.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** people who love deduction and guessing identities; icebreakers; newcomers and grandparents can learn it instantly; short gaps — waiting for friends, before or after dinner
- **Not ideal for:** no obvious bad fit
