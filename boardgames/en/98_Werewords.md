## Overview

20 questions plus werewolves: the mayor answers yes/no; werewolves know the word and mislead; the seer must stay hidden.

## Setup

1. Use the app; deal roles; the mayor picks a word; werewolf and seer learn it.

## How to play

1. Players ask yes/no questions; the mayor answers with tokens.
2. Guess within time.

## Game end & scoring

- Word guessed: werewolves try to find the seer.
- Not guessed: everyone votes for a werewolf.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** Word guessed: werewolves try to find the seer.
2. **What you do on a turn:** Players ask yes/no questions; the mayor answers with tokens.
3. **Setup check:** Use the app; deal roles; the mayor picks a word; werewolf and seer learn it.
4. **The one rule people forget:** The mayor communicates only with tokens.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- Seer guides subtly.
- Werewolves waste time with good-sounding questions.
- 'So close' tokens are information.

## Common mistakes

- The mayor communicates only with tokens.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** big parties; people who love deduction and guessing identities; icebreakers; newcomers and grandparents can learn it instantly; short gaps — waiting for friends, before or after dinner
- **Not ideal for:** no obvious bad fit

### First-game time budget

- Teaching: about 5 min (weight ●).
- Play: 10–10 min; budget about 10 min for a first game.
- Total: about 15 min.

### Teaching script (read it aloud)

> Twenty questions plus werewolves. The mayor picks a word and answers yes/no. Werewolves know it and mislead; the seer knows and guides. If guessed, werewolves try to find the seer; if not, villagers try to find a werewolf.

### Strategy by phase

- **Early:** Ask category questions first.
- **Mid:** Watch for too-accurate or misleading questions.
- **Late:** Seer stay low-key.
