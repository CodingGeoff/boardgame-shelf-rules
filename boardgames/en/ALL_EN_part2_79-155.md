# Brass: Birmingham

> Chinese name: **工业革命 Brass：伯明翰** · Doc #79 · [中文原文 / Chinese doc](../79_%E5%B7%A5%E4%B8%9A%E9%9D%A9%E5%91%BD_Brass.md) · [Open in the web app](https://codinggeoff.github.io/boardgame-shelf-rules/#doc/79)

| Play time | 60–120 min |
| Age | 14+ |
| Complexity | ●●●●○ Medium-heavy (4/5) |
| Tags | economic, network, heavy, hand management |

## Where is it on the shelf?

- **Shelf code:** none on the photo — inferred area: ≈ top of the D/E "free to borrow" cabinet; ≈ top of the E–G cabinets (above the E1/E2, F1/F2, G1/G2 signs) *(inferred)*

## Overview

The Industrial Revolution over the canal and rail eras: build industries, links and sell goods using loans.

## Setup

1. Set up the market, merchants and cards; each player has a board and money.

## How to play

1. Two actions per turn (one in the first): build, network, develop, sell, loan, scout.
2. Coal and iron consumption follow connection rules.
3. Turn order: least money spent goes first.

## Game end & scoring

- After each era score links and flipped industries; the canal era removes canals and level-1 industries; most points wins.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** After each era score links and flipped industries; the canal era removes canals and level-1 industries; most points wins.
2. **What you do on a turn:** Two actions per turn (one in the first): build, network, develop, sell, loan, scout.
3. **Setup check:** Set up the market, merchants and cards; each player has a board and money.
4. **The one rule people forget:** Loans reduce income level.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- Cotton and pottery need merchants to flip.
- Use others' coal and iron.
- Claim key routes in the canal era.

## Common mistakes

- Loans reduce income level.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** experienced gamers who want a deep think
- **Not ideal for:** relaxed chatting sessions

# Night of the Ninja

> Chinese name: **忍者之夜** · Doc #80 · [中文原文 / Chinese doc](../80_%E5%BF%8D%E8%80%85%E4%B9%8B%E5%A4%9C_Night_of_the_Ninja.md) · [Open in the web app](https://codinggeoff.github.io/boardgame-shelf-rules/#doc/80)

- Original note (Chinese): 第三轮照片 155102：D1/D2 层（标签 E1 / E2 / F2） → 忍者之夜 Night of the Ninja

## Overview

Lotus and Crane clans assassinate each other: draft ninja cards, act in the night, then reveal.

## Setup

1. Take clan cards by player count; each player draws a secret clan card and 3 ninja cards.

## How to play

1. Draft: keep 1, pass 2; repeat.
2. Night: resolve in card number order (spy, mystic, trickster, assassin…).
3. Reveal and score.

## Game end & scoring

- Clan scores when the opponents' highest-ranked member is killed; the Ronin and others have own goals; play to the point target.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** Clan scores when the opponents' highest-ranked member is killed; the Ronin and others have own goals; play to the point target.
2. **What you do on a turn:** Draft: keep 1, pass 2; repeat.
3. **Setup check:** Take clan cards by player count; each player draws a secret clan card and 3 ninja cards.
4. **The one rule people forget:** Odd counts add the Ronin.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- What you pass is information.
- Reveal identities selectively.
- Assassinate the top-ranked enemy.

## Common mistakes

- Odd counts add the Ronin.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** big parties; people who love deduction and guessing identities
- **Not ideal for:** no obvious bad fit

# Decrypto

> Chinese name: **截码战 Decrypto** · Doc #81 · [中文原文 / Chinese doc](../81_%E6%88%AA%E7%A0%81%E6%88%98_Decrypto.md) · [Open in the web app](https://codinggeoff.github.io/boardgame-shelf-rules/#doc/81)

|---|---|
| Players | 3–8 (best: 6-8) |
| Play time | 15–45 min |
| Age | 12+ |

## Where is it on the shelf?

- **Shelf code:** D1 / D2 / E1 / E2 / F2
- **Photos:** [155102](../../photos/web/IMG20261127155102.jpg)
- Original note (Chinese): 第三轮照片 155102：D1/D2 层（标签 E1 / E2 / F2） → 截码战 Decrypto

## Overview

Two teams each have 4 secret keywords and transmit 3-digit codes with clues; the other team tries to intercept.

## Setup

1. Two screens; each team places 4 keyword cards in slots 1–4 visible only to them.

## How to play

1. An encryptor draws a code (e.g. 3-1-4) and gives 3 clues.
2. Their team guesses the code; the opponents try to intercept (not in round 1).
3. Miscommunication earns a white token; interceptions earn black tokens.

## Game end & scoring

- 2 interceptions win; 2 miscommunications lose; tiebreaks per rules after 8 rounds.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** 2 interceptions win; 2 miscommunications lose; tiebreaks per rules after 8 rounds.
2. **What you do on a turn:** An encryptor draws a code (e.g. 3-1-4) and gives 3 clues.
3. **Setup check:** Two screens; each team places 4 keyword cards in slots 1–4 visible only to them.
4. **The one rule people forget:** No interceptions in round 1.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- Use inside jokes only your team knows.
- Vary association types.
- Log the opponents' clues per number.

## Common mistakes

- No interceptions in round 1.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** big parties; people who love deduction and guessing identities
- **Not ideal for:** no obvious bad fit

- Place the largest or oddest piece first.
- Both levels must be complete.
- Start with 3-piece sides.

## Common mistakes

- Exactly two levels, no holes or overhang.

# Dixit (with Stella)

## Overview

A storyteller gives a clue for a picture; others add matching pictures; everyone votes for the storyteller's card.

## Setup

1. Deal 6 picture cards each; rabbits on the score track; voting tokens.

## How to play

1. The storyteller picks a card and gives a clue (word, sentence, sound).
2. Others give a matching card face down; shuffle and reveal.
3. Everyone except the storyteller votes.

## Game end & scoring

- If all or nobody find the storyteller's card, others score 2 and the storyteller 0; otherwise storyteller and correct voters score 3; extra votes on your card score 1.

## Overview

Michael Schacht's betting-and-dice delivery race: bet a number 2–12, the highest bidder must roll it on 2 dice, then deliver sushi around a ring to collect customer sets.

## Setup

1. Set up the ring route (sushi shop and park spaces) and the betting board (2–12).
2. Each player's van starts at the sushi shop with 2 fixed action cards (Same Bet, One Extra Step); shuffle remaining action cards.
3. Reveal customer cards equal to the number of players.

## How to play

1. Bet: place your stone on an empty number (2–12); next round the lowest bidder bets first.
2. Challenge: the highest bidder rolls 2 dice up to twice and must reach at least their bet. Success: they and all lower bidders move. Failure: they take an action card as consolation and the next-highest tries.
3. Move by your bet, highest first. Reaching or passing the sushi shop takes 1 face-up customer card (max 1 per move); stopping exactly on the park takes an action card.
4. Refill: the lowest bidder decides whether leftover customers stay or are cleared; refill to player count.
5. Action cards: same bet, swap adjacent bets, reroll a die, one extra step, extra tip.

## Game end & scoring

- Ends when customer cards cannot be refilled.
- Each customer colour: first card 3 points, each additional 1 point (rare colours have only 2 cards).
- The player with the fewest tip cards loses points (amount per rulebook); highest total wins.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** Ends when customer cards cannot be refilled.
2. **What you do on a turn:** Bet: place your stone on an empty number (2–12); next round the lowest bidder bets first.
3. **Setup check:** Set up the ring route (sushi shop and park spaces) and the betting board (2–12).
4. **The one rule people forget:** Only the highest bidder rolls; lower bidders move if they succeed.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- High bets move far but risk failure.
- Rare colours are worth chasing.
- Do not ignore tips.

## Common mistakes

- Only the highest bidder rolls; lower bidders move if they succeed.
- At most one customer card per move.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** most board gamers
- **Not ideal for:** no obvious bad fit
