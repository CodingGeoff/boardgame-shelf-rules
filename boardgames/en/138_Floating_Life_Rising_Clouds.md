## Overview

Chinese-style light strategy (Yaofish Games): scholars rise and fall in officialdom; roll and move exactly, take tiles you leave empty, and use koi tiles to flip negatives.

## Setup

1. Components: 8 fortune tiles, 18 adversity tiles, start and end tiles, 32 floating-life tiles, 18 scholars, 8 guards, 1 die.
2. Lay out the path from start to end; place scholars and guards per rulebook.

## How to play

1. Roll one six-sided die.
2. Choose one, moving exactly the rolled number: move one of your scholars — if the tile you leave has no scholars or guards, you MUST take it as points (positive or negative); or move a guard that shares a tile with any scholar — no points, but it changes who empties tiles.

## Game end & scoring

- The game ends immediately when all scholars reach the end.
- Sum positive and negative tiles; each koi tile flips one negative tile to equal positive (e.g. −6 → +6).
- Highest total wins; variant rules are included.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** The game ends immediately when all scholars reach the end.
2. **What you do on a turn:** Roll one six-sided die.
3. **Setup check:** Components: 8 fortune tiles, 18 adversity tiles, start and end tiles, 32 floating-life tiles, 18 scholars, 8 guards, 1 die.
4. **The one rule people forget:** Movement must use the full roll.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- Guards can force opponents to take bad tiles.
- Save koi for your biggest negative.
- Count exact distances.

## Common mistakes

- Movement must use the full roll.
- Taking an emptied tile is mandatory.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** icebreakers; newcomers and grandparents can learn it instantly
- **Not ideal for:** no obvious bad fit
