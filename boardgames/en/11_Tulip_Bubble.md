## Overview

17th-century tulip mania: speculate through events and auctions, then cash out before the bubble bursts.

## Setup

1. Tulips in 3 colours, each with grades A/B/C; each colour has a price track (1–7).
2. Shuffle the Bubble Bust card into the last 3 event cards.
3. Each player takes starting money and 3 bidding markers.

## How to play

1. Event phase: reveal an event that changes prices or demand.
2. Sell phase: sell tulips to the bank at market price (price × grade) or to collectors who match their requirements.
3. Buy phase: bid for tulip lots with your markers; you may take loans (max 3).
4. Price adjustment based on trading and events.

## Game end & scoring

- Black tulip worth 120 wins instantly (see rulebook).
- When Bubble Bust appears, the market crashes: unsold loan-bought tulips become debt; most cash wins.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** Black tulip worth 120 wins instantly (see rulebook).
2. **What you do on a turn:** Event phase: reveal an event that changes prices or demand.
3. **Setup check:** Tulips in 3 colours, each with grades A/B/C; each colour has a price track (1–7).
4. **The one rule people forget:** Max 3 loans each; unsold financed tulips at the crash become debt.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- With 3 event cards left, start selling — especially loan-financed tulips.
- Collectors often pay better than the market.
- Deny opponents close to the black tulip.

## Common mistakes

- Max 3 loans each; unsold financed tulips at the crash become debt.
- Adjust prices only per rules, not by feel.

### Worked example (illustrative)

1. Event: 'red demand rises' — red price up 1.
2. Sell: I sell a red grade-A tulip to the bank (price × grade).
3. Auction: I bid on a yellow lot, financing with a loan token.
4. With 3 events left everyone sells — the bust could come any time.

### Scoring example

- After the bust, unsold loan-bought tulips become debt; most cash wins, or a 120-value black tulip wins instantly.
