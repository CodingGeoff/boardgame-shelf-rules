## Overview

A two-player bakumatsu spy duel: six face-down agents with hidden values and real or fake intel move with location cards and capture by collision.

## Setup

1. Each side has 6 agents: 3 (real), 3 (fake), 2 (real), 2 (fake), 1 (fake), 0 (real); real intel shows a red dot.
2. Place 5 agents on your back row and 1 in front of the middle space, values facing you.
3. Shuffle the location and tactic decks; each player draws 4 location + 2 tactic cards (6 total).

## How to play

1. Play 1 card, then refill to 6 from either deck.
2. Location cards: move one agent 1 space forward or diagonally forward onto a space of the card's colour (wild cards excepted). No sideways or backward moves.
3. Your agents cannot share a space and cannot enter the enemy HQ unless an ability says so.
4. Conflict: moving onto an enemy agent makes you the attacker. The defender reveals; the attacker states its value truthfully without revealing.
5. Compare: 3 > 2 > 1 > 0, but 0 beats 3. The attacker wins on greater or equal and captures the defender.
6. Tactic cards (e.g. Defence) allow special moves such as stepping sideways or back.

## Game end & scoring

- Win by capturing enough real intel, or by getting a real-intel agent into the enemy HQ (see rulebook for exact conditions).
- Advanced play adds charm and equipment cards.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** Win by capturing enough real intel, or by getting a real-intel agent into the enemy HQ (see rulebook for exact conditions).
2. **What you do on a turn:** Play 1 card, then refill to 6 from either deck.
3. **Setup check:** Each side has 6 agents: 3 (real), 3 (fake), 2 (real), 2 (fake), 1 (fake), 0 (real); real intel shows a red dot.
4. **The one rule people forget:** Location cards only allow moves onto matching colours.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- 0 beats 3 — hide your 0 where the enemy 3 will travel.
- Attackers only announce values, so attacking keeps your real/fake status secret.
- Save Defence-type tactics for when an agent is cornered.

## Common mistakes

- Location cards only allow moves onto matching colours.
- Check the rulebook for what happens when both decks run out.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** couples / head-to-head duels; talkative groups who enjoy acting and bluffing; people who love deduction and guessing identities
- **Not ideal for:** players who dislike conflict or backstabbing

### Worked example (illustrative)

1. My turn: I hold 4 location and 2 tactic cards. I play a red location card and move my middle agent one space diagonally forward onto red.
2. Draw 1 from the location deck to refill to 6.
3. Opponent's turn: his agent moves onto my agent's space — a conflict. I defend and reveal first: '2 (real)'; his is '3 (fake)'.
4. Values are compared; the higher attacker wins and my agent is captured.
5. He has captured one real intel; I now try to sneak my remaining real-intel agents into his HQ.

### Scoring example

- Win per the rulebook: typically capture 3 enemy real intel, get a real-intel agent into the enemy HQ, or leave the opponent with only fakes.

### Rules FAQ / rulings

- **Q: Can I move sideways or back?**  
  A: No — only forward or diagonally forward onto the card's colour (wild cards excepted).
- **Q: Are captured agents revealed?**  
  A: Yes, conflicts reveal values — that's part of the information game.

### Player-count differences

- Strictly 2 players.
- Best with: 2 players.

### Official variants & expansions

- For a first game use location cards only, then add tactic cards.

## Advanced guide: teaching script, time budget, phase strategy, extra FAQ

> Summarised by the editor from the rules above for teaching and improving; the rulebook wins on conflicts.

### First-game time budget

- Teaching: about 10 min (weight ●●).
- Play: 15–30 min; budget about 35 min for a first game.
- Total: about 45 min.

### Teaching script (read it aloud)

> Each side has 6 agents facing away from the opponent, each showing a strength number or a true/false intel mark. On your turn play a movement card to move one agent; landing on an enemy agent captures it and reveals it. Collect enough of the opponent's real intel (or meet the other win conditions on the rules sheet) to win. Movement cards cycle: once used they come back after the set is spent.

### Strategy by phase

- **Early:** Keep real intel in the back and big numbers forward; probe with small agents.
- **Mid:** Track the opponent's spent movement cards to know where they can reach next.
- **Late:** When ahead, protect your intel; when behind, bait captures with fake intel.

### Extra FAQ

- **Q: What if I capture fake intel?**  
  A: Check the win conditions on the rules sheet — capturing fakes can backfire.
- **Q: Can I pass?**  
  A: Normally you must play a card and move; see the rulebook.
