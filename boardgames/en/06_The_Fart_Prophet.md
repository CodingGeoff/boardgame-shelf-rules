## Overview

A comedic team deduction game in an elevator: a hidden Farter team knows which floors stink; everyone holds breath, accuses and votes over 8 floors.

## Setup

1. Take identity and environment cards for the player count; each player gets a speaking-order card and a secret identity.
2. Each player draws 2 character cards and keeps 1; set health to 4.
3. A dealer lays out 8 face-down environment cards (one per floor); the Farter team sees which floors have farts.

## How to play

1. Decide whether to play an action card face down (not playing means no action this round).
2. Reveal this floor's environment card and resolve actions in order (protect, hold breath, accuse…).
3. On fart floors: affected players lose health, then everyone speaks and points at a suspect on three; most votes is eliminated regardless of health.
4. Ties revote between the tied players; another tie means nobody leaves.
5. Once 2+ players are out, an eliminated player may secretly check identities (see rulebook).

## Game end & scoring

- A team wins immediately when the other team is fully eliminated.
- After 8 floors, the team with more survivors wins.

## 5-minute teaching order

1. **Goal first:** A team wins immediately when the other team is fully eliminated.
2. **What you do on a turn:** Decide whether to play an action card face down (not playing means no action this round).
3. **Setup check:** Take identity and environment cards for the player count; each player gets a speaking-order card and a secret identity.
4. **The one rule people forget:** Players at 0 health are out and cannot speak.
5. Play one open-handed practice round, then start for real.

## Strategy tips

- Farters: reacting perfectly every time gives you away.
- Good players: track who always seems prepared for fart floors.
- Advanced supernatural identities add twists.

## Common mistakes

- Players at 0 health are out and cannot speak.
- Vote ties are re-voted once.

## Good for / not for

- **Good for:** big parties; people who love deduction and guessing identities
- **Not ideal for:** no obvious bad fit
